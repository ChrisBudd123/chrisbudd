  • 31st January   Tam Smith and Ben Sparks   Knowing the unknowable


    • We invite students to explore how mathematicians tackle problems that seem impossible to answer. Through hands-on activities, live demonstrations, and surprising real-world examples, students will discover how mathematical techniques can reveal hidden truths. From estimating the uncountable, to safely asking unaskable questions, to making sense of chance itself and guessing the unguessable, this interactive masterclass shows how mathematical thinking turns uncertainty into insight.



  • 7th February   Ben Adams   Random walks

    Choose a direction at random. Face in that direction. Take one step forward. Choose another direction at random, take one step forward. Repeat x 10. Where do you end up? Is the outcome the same every time you do this random walk? How often to you return to where you started? We'll use maths to explore these questions, and make serendipitous connections between stock markets, stink bombs and albatrosses. If you have a 6 sided die, please bring it along. If you don't have one, don't worry - we've got plenty to share.

  • 28th February   Sam Brzezicki   Nim … and turtles?

    Nim, a classic game in an area known as combinatorial game theory, is a two-player turn based game involving several piles of counters. On a player's turn they must choose a pile and remove some (at least one but as many as all) counters from that pile. The winner of the game is the player who removes the final counter(s) from the game. In this session we will play Nim and we'll figure out how to do that well! Turning Turtles is a game involving a line of turtles in which players take turns to flip over turtles in a certain way - a seemingly very different game to Nim but is there a connection between them? Come and find out! This masterclass will be enjoyed by anyone with a love for games, maths or turtles! PS - No turtles will be harmed during this masterclass.




  • 7th March   Illaria Bussoli   Counting Success and More

    Have you ever wondered how is it decided if a cough syrup really helps with your cough and is really different from all other brands on the market? Or how is it determined if a particular player or athlete is more performant than all others? In this masterclass, we will answer these and more questions by actively engaging with one of the core tools of statistical investigation: hypothesis testing. Through hands-on exploration, you will learn about random variables and their sampling distributions, practice formulating your own hypotheses, and use probability to make smart decisions when testing them.

  • 14th March   James Davenport   Cryptography

    We will discuss examples of cryptography (secret writing) from history (Julius Caesar) and fiction (Sherlock Holmes), and the students will work on an example. These depend on the participants having an agreed secret code. But these days we want to go to an online shop and buy secretly (certainly the credit card details, even if not the "day return to Bristol") when we don't have any agreed secret. We demonstrate how this is possible, and the students then work on another example. We conclude with more modern cryptography, notably digital signatures.
